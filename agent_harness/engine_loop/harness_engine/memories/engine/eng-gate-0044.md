[engine] A printed field's delimiter is part of its contract, not cosmetic: if a reader is documented to recognise `key=[...]`, the writer must print the brackets, and a liberal reader that accepts the bare form as well is a compatibility convenience rather than a licence to print either. The failure mode is specific and nasty -- the reader reports 'field absent', which is indistinguishable from 'the producer was never enabled', while the producer's own banner a few lines earlier says it is ACTIVE. Whenever a tool can report 'the instrument is missing', that report must be distinguishable from 'the instrument ran and I could not read it', or every absence verdict becomes unfalsifiable.

## 為什麼（because）

The slot->expert annotation was added to the pool-row capture and documented as `own=[...]`, with `own=none` reserved for 'not annotated'. The dumper printed a bare comma list. The comparator, written against the documentation, therefore found no owner on any row and printed its loud 'OWNER CHANNEL ABSENT -- no CONTENT rows must not be read as the content axis was cleared' warning, on two runs whose ggml-metal banner said `owner channel ACTIVE` in the same log. The first reading of a brand-new instrument was a false alarm about the instrument itself, and the run had to be repeated after a rebuild.

## 反例（counterexample_observed）

Run 2026-09-17 11:0x: the comparator printed OWNER CHANNEL ABSENT for /tmp/r13_own1.log, whose line 38 reads `CGC-POOL-CAP: owner channel ACTIVE (CGC-IDS-CAP rows will carry own=[...])`. After printing brackets, the same logs give `owner column: 210 of 210 POOL rows carry own=`. The comparator now also accepts the bare form, and a truncated `own=[5,9` (no closing bracket) is deliberately still treated as NOT annotated rather than as a short list.
